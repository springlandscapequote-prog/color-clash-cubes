        Color Clash Cubes — Invented by Gabriela Rubio
                           (2025)
    Game Concept Overview
    Color Clash Cubes is a creative, family-friendly building and strategy game invented by Gabriela Rubio.
    The game blends luck, color collection, and construction challenges into a competitive race to build and
    protect colored cubes. It encourages counting, spatial awareness, and strategic thinking for children
    and adults alike.

    Game Components
    • 72 interlocking connector pieces, shaped like hash (#) symbols, each color having 12 pieces: Yellow,
    Teal, Light Purple, Burgundy Purple, Blue, and Red. • 2 standard dice (numbered 1–6) • 2 color cubes
    (each side painted one of the six game colors) • Rule card or booklet • Box and insert for storage

    Objective of the Game
    Players roll dice to collect colored pieces and compete to complete cubes made of six matching
    connectors. Each finished cube becomes 'safe' and cannot be stolen. The player with the most
    completed cubes at the end wins.

    How to Play
    1. On your turn, roll one number die and one color cube. 2. Take the number of pieces shown on the
    number die in the color shown on the cube. 3. If you collect 6 of the same color, you assemble them
    into a cube — this cube is now safe and cannot be stolen. 4. If a color runs out in the shared pool,
    players may take from other players’ unsafe pieces of that color. 5. The game ends when no legal
    moves remain or all cubes are completed.

    Example of Gameplay
    • Player 1 rolls a 6 and Blue → collects 6 blue pieces and builds a complete blue cube (safe). • Player 2
    rolls a 3 and Teal → takes 3 teal pieces (unsafe until 6 are collected). • Later, when blue pieces run low,
    if Player 3 rolls a 5 and Blue, but only 2 remain in the pool, they may take the 2 remaining plus 3 blue
    pieces from another player’s unsafe pile. • Only completed cubes are safe and locked.

    Educational and Developmental Benefits
    • Promotes math skills (counting, probability) • Encourages color recognition and sorting • Teaches
    resource management and turn-based play • Strengthens hand-eye coordination and spatial reasoning
    • Supports social skills like patience and sportsmanship
